# BS Zelda Series

## Overview

The **BS Zelda** series consists of three distinct Satellaview games based on *The Legend of Zelda: A Link to the Past*. These games were among the most popular Satellaview titles and have received significant fan restoration efforts.[^1]

## BS Zelda no Densetsu (Map 1: "Third Quest")

The first BS Zelda game featured a modified version of A Link to the Past's overworld with new dungeon layouts. The dungeon names spell **"St.GIGA"**:[^2]

| Week | Dungeon Theme |
|------|---------------|
| Week 1 | S |
| Week 2 | t |
| Week 3 | . |
| Week 4 | GIGA |

Each week was broadcast separately and required the player to complete the dungeon within the broadcast period. The game was downloadable and could be played between broadcasts.[^3]

## BS Zelda no Densetsu (Map 2: "Fourth Quest")

The sequel map featured even harder dungeons. The dungeon names spell **"NiNtENDO"**:[^4]

| Week | Dungeon Theme |
|------|---------------|
| Week 1 | N |
| Week 2 | i |
| Week 3 | N |
| Week 4 | tENDO |

## BS Zelda no Densetsu: Inishie no Sekiban (Ancient Stone Tablets)

The most complex of the BS Zelda titles, **Ancient Stone Tablets (AST)** was a **SoundLink game** -- the first Zelda game with full voice acting and a live radio drama component. The game was broadcast in 1997-1998 as the centerpiece of St.GIGA's Satellaview programming.[^5]

### Format
- **SoundLink format** -- required live radio broadcast; could not be saved to Memory Pack
- Featured voice actors performing character dialogue in real time
- Music and sound effects synchronized with radio broadcast
- Functioned as a direct sequel to A Link to the Past[^6]

### Story
The game takes place six years after A Link to the Past. A new villain named **Grah** appears and Link must once again save Hyrule. The game introduced new items, puzzles, and a scoring system that ranked players based on performance.[^7]

### Scoring System
- Deaths: -10,000 points each
- Rupees: +10 points each
- Heart Containers and tablets auto-calculated
- Result screens at end of each week calculated scores automatically[^8]

## Restoration History

### ROM Dumping
The BS Zelda ROMs have a complex dumping history. The first known dumps came from **BS-X Cult** (via Pachuka/ROMar) and were among the first Satellaview ROMs to hit the internet. Multiple revisions and versions have been identified; some versions have undumped corruption (e.g., week 3 of Map 1 had `0x28CC7: 0x02 instead of 0x00` per LuigiBlood's analysis).[^9]

### BS Zelda Shrine (1999-2020)
The **BS Zelda Shrine**, founded November 2, 1999 by Con, has been the primary hub for BS Zelda restoration. Key milestones:

- Full restoration of Map 1 and Map 2 as real-hardware-compatible ROMs[^10]
- English translation of in-game text
- Voice-over subtitle patches
- MSU-1 audio enhancement patches
- Master Quest hard mode (2009)
- AST Master Quest competition (April 2009)[^11]

### Translation Efforts
The BS Zelda games featured voice-over dialogue that was never subtitled. Fan translation projects have worked to:
- Transcribe all voice-over material
- Create English subtitle patches
- Translate Japanese text in menus and item descriptions[^12]

### Music Restoration
The BS Zelda music has been restored from multiple sources:
- **VHS recordings** of original broadcasts (audio extraction and splicing)[^13]
- **Album sources** -- official soundtrack releases that contained BS Zelda music[^14]
- **MSU-1 patches** -- replacing restoration-enhanced music in the ROMs[^15]

## Current Status (2026)

- All three BS Zelda games are playable in emulation
- Map 1 Week 4 was newly dumped as recently as December 2020 (from sanmaiwashi/LuigiBlood)[^16]
- Con declared the restoration mission complete in 2016 but returned for minor updates through 2020[^17]
- The BS Zelda Shrine remains online at bszelda.zeldalegends.net
- The forum (originally at z9.invisionfree.com/bszelda) is largely defunct
