# Fan Projects Overview

Since the time of the last Satellaview broadcast, numerous fan sites and projects have emerged to memorialize the system and preserve its content. Due to the Satellaview's Japan-only release, the overwhelming majority of early fan sites were Japanese-language.[^1]

## The Preservation Challenge

The dissolution of St.GIGA and Nintendo's attempts to distance itself from what has been considered a failed system, combined with the inevitable progress of subsequent technologies, has resulted in much of the information that once existed on the system being lost to time. Modern sites serve quasi-archeological purposes and engage in historical research.[^2]

## Community Structure

The Satellaview community can be divided into several overlapping groups:

| Community Type | Description |
|----------------|-------------|
| **Historical Japanese** | Sites from the active broadcast era (1995-2000) and immediate post-service period |
| **Modern Japanese** | Blogs and wikis maintained by Japanese enthusiasts documenting the system |
| **English-Language** | Primarily the Satellablog, BS Zelda Shrine, and English Satellaview Wiki |
| **Technical/Preservation** | ROM dumping, emulation, signal simulation, and restoration projects |

## Key Principles

The community generally operates under shared values:
- Restoration should aim for accuracy to original materials (though disagreements exist)
- Content should be shared freely, not sold for profit
- Profiteering (e.g., eBay sales of restored material) is widely condemned[^3]
- Communication between language groups is encouraged but often challenging[^4]

## Restoration Approaches

Restoration groups can be divided into:
1. **Purists** -- seek to restore original material in as accurate-to-the-original a manner as possible
2. **Innovators** -- seek to restore a semblance of the original game, sometimes incorporating modern innovations
3. **Expansionists** -- introduce Satellaview elements to non-Satellaview games (e.g., inserting the BS-X avatar into A Link to the Past)[^5]

## Current State (2026)

- **The Satellablog** (superfamicom.org/blog) is actively maintained by danyl, posting regular dumps
- **BS-X Project** remains in maintenance mode with the latest SatellaWave release in 2019
- **Satellaview+** offers simulated live broadcasts
- **Elude Visibility** continues to release new Satellaview dumps
- **Japanese sites** are mostly inactive but archived
