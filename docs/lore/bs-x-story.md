# BS-X: The Story of the Town Whose Name Was Stolen

![BS-X town map from Satellaview History Museum](../assets/images/museum/sfc_sv.gif){ align="right" width="300" }

## Overview

**BS-X: Sore wa Namae o Nusumareta Machi no Monogatari** (BS-X: それは名前を盗まれた街の物語, "BS-X: The Story of the Town Whose Name Was Stolen") was the operating system and user interface cartridge for the Satellaview system. Rather than a conventional menu, it presented itself as a fully realized game world -- a small town that the player could explore, populated by quirky characters, and filled with interactive elements.[^1]

The game was developed by **Nintendo** in collaboration with **St.GIGA** and was released on **April 23, 1995** as a launch title for the Satellaview system.[^2]

## Premise

The player character arrives at a town whose name has been stolen by a mysterious entity. The townspeople cannot remember what the town was called. The player's task is to explore the town, interact with its residents, and participate in events to gradually restore the town's identity -- all while using the town as a hub to access Satellaview's broadcast features.[^3]

The game's central location is **Ransei Bridge** (らんせい橋), where the player begins their journey. From there, they can access all areas of the town.[^4]

## Gameplay

Unlike a traditional game, BS-X functioned as a **graphical operating system**:

### Navigation
The player moved their avatar through a 2D top-down town using the D-pad. The town was divided into several districts, each offering different services:[^5]

- **Event Plaza** - Time-limited downloadable events and content
- **Download Center** - Browse and download broadcasted games and data
- **Memory Pack Manager** - Manage saved content on the Memory Pack
- **Magazine Stand** - Access downloadable game magazines and news
- **Save/Configuration** - Player name, gender, and system settings

### Player Avatar
Players configured their **name and gender** at the start. This determined the appearance of their in-game avatar:[^6]

- **Male** - Character wearing a backward baseball cap and a blue outfit
- **Female** - Character with a pink ponytail and a pink/white outfit

This avatar would carry into many Satellaview broadcast games where supported, including the *BS Zelda* series and *BS Fire Emblem*.[^7]

### Real-Time Features
BS-X was designed around the Satellaview's broadcast schedule. The town changed based on:

- **Time of day** - Different characters appeared at different hours
- **Day of week** - Certain events were only available on specific days
- **Broadcast schedule** - Downloadable content appeared during and after broadcasts
- **Seasonal events** - Holiday-themed content was broadcast periodically[^8]

## The Satellaview Guide

In later years (1997-1999), St.GIGA shifted to broadcasting primarily **Satellaview Guide** content -- a service that provided news, game tips, and magazine-style articles rather than full game downloads. This was reflected in the BS-X interface through updated town content.[^9]

## The Name and Identity Theme

The central metaphor of BS-X -- a town whose name has been stolen -- reflects the ephemeral nature of the Satellaview service itself. The town exists in a state of lost identity, much like the system's content would later become "lost" after broadcasts ceased. This theme has resonated strongly with preservationists working to recover and document the system's history.[^10]

## BS-X in Emulation

The BS-X cartridge was initially difficult to emulate due to its custom mapper and the need for satellite signal simulation. The first BS-X BIOS emulation became available on **September 24, 2004** via custom emulator builds.[^11]

**LuigiBlood's BS-X Project** (started 2010) was the first comprehensive attempt to fake a satellite signal, allowing the BS-X cartridge to function in emulators as if receiving live broadcasts. The project produced:[^12]

- An English translation patch (final version v1.3, 2016)
- The SatellaWave server manager for creating broadcast data
- Emulation support in bsnes-plus and SNES9X
- sd2snes/FX Pak Pro real-hardware support

## Legacy

BS-X remains one of the most distinctive operating systems in video game history -- a full game world that also functioned as a console's dashboard. Its approach of embedding system functionality within a narrative setting was unique for its time and remains rare to this day. The complete source code for the BS-X town interface has never been released, and the game survives today only through ROM dumps of retail cartridges and fan preservation efforts.[^13]
